3CampusConnect · Product & Architecture Blueprint
0101
E X E C U T I V E  S U M M A R Y
An operating layer for inter-college student life
CampusConnect turns thousands of isolated campus communities into one verified, searchable and
collaborative network, and gives every community the tools to actually do something together once
they've found each other.
The problem (PS 2)
Student communities operate in isolation. Finding
relevant organizations at other colleges is hard.
Promotion stops at the campus gate, inside single-
campus WhatsApp groups. Collaborations depend on
whoever has personal contacts this year.
Our core insight
Isolation isn't caused by a missing feed. Three systems
are missing: (1) a way to find a complementary partner,
(2) a low-friction way to plan and run things together,
and (3) institutional memory that survives the annual
leadership turnover. Generic social networks offer none
of the three.
The solution
Five pillars (Discover · Connect · Promote ·
Collaborate · Sustain) delivered through 12 features,
including AI partner matching (Collab Compass),
clash-aware scheduling (Date Doctor), poster-to-event
publishing (Event Launchpad), shared co-host
workspaces (Collab Rooms) and leadership-handover
memory (Legacy Vault).
Built to scale
A modular monolith that grows into event-driven
services. PostgreSQL is the source of truth, and search,
feeds and analytics are rebuildable projections. It runs
on AWS Mumbai with multi-AZ redundancy and a
Hyderabad DR region. Capacity-planned for 1M DAU
and spike-safe for fest registration rushes.
How we beat the cold start. A social network is useless with no users, but CampusConnect isn't only a social
network. A single club gets value on day 1: it publishes events from a poster in 30 seconds, syndicates them to
WhatsApp and Instagram, checks dates against public calendars and keeps its Legacy Vault. Each of those actions
adds a node to the network, and every shared link and collab request invites the next one.
What judges should remember
Complete PS coverage
Every sentence of PS 2 maps to a named
feature and a metric (§03).
Novel, not a clone
Complementarity matching, clash
detection, resource exchange and
leadership-handover memory don't exist
together in any student platform we
know of.
Production-grade engineering
Outbox pattern, CDC, cell-based scale
path, SLOs, DR, DPDP compliance, all
specified in detail.
India-first
WhatsApp-first distribution, Hindi +
regional languages, Lite mode for low-
end phones, data stored in India.
Institution value
College HQ gives approvals, safety
oversight and collaboration records
useful for accreditation documentation.
A business
Free for students. Revenue from College
HQ licences, a Sponsor Pool, community
Pro plans and ticketing fees.

Features that don't exist anywhere else
Each one targets a root cause from §02 and has a concrete implementation path, so none of it is hand-
waving.
Collab Compass
AI partner matching built on complementarity, not
similarity.
PROBLEMPartners are found through personal contacts, and
cold DMs go unanswered.
HOWEvery community has a skills profile (what it's good at)
and a goals profile (what it wants to do this term). Compass
recommends partners whose skills complement yours, whose
goals overlap, who are nearby and who are trusted, and it
shows why: "They bring UI/UX and branding; you bring ML.
Both plan a November hackathon. 6 km apart."
pgvector embeddings · explainable match score (§12) · weights
learned from completed collaborations
01 Date Doctor
Scheduling that knows what clashes with your event.
PROBLEMNearby colleges schedule big events on the same
weekend and split the audience.
HOWWhen picking a date, organizers see a heat strip of
overlapping events for the same audience within a radius,
plus known exam windows and festivals. They get the three
best slots, each with a reason.
PostGIS radius × audience-overlap (interest embeddings) ·
academic calendars from College HQ · per-day clash score
02
Event Launchpad
From poster to live event in 30 seconds.
PROBLEMClubs already make a poster. Retyping details into
every platform is what kills promotion.
HOWDrop a poster, Instagram link or WhatsApp text. AI
extracts the title, date, venue, fee, eligibility and registration
link, and highlights low-confidence fields for confirmation.
Publishing auto-generates an OG card, a QR code and
bilingual descriptions.
multimodal LLM with JSON-schema output · per-field confidence ·
geocoding · human-in-the-loop confirmation
03 Syndication Bridge
Reach students where they already are.
PROBLEMIndian students live on WhatsApp and Instagram,
not on yet another app.
HOWPublish once, then push to WhatsApp Channels,
Instagram, LinkedIn and email digests. Every share carries a
tracked short link, so organizers see which college and
channel drove registrations.
WhatsApp Cloud API + Meta Graph connectors · short-link service
· attribution in ClickHouse
04
Collab Room + Charter
A shared workspace the moment two clubs say yes.
PROBLEMCo-hosting runs in chaotic group chats, and
responsibilities and money get lost.
HOWAccepting a collab request auto-creates a room with a
task board, cross-org roles, a budget-split ledger, files and a
joint registration page. It also produces a one-page COLLAB
CHARTER (who does what, logo placement, revenue split)
that both sides approve.
real-time sync over WebSockets · charter rendered to PDF and
stored as a signed, versioned record
05 Resource Exchange
Share what you have, borrow what you lack.
PROBLEMResource asymmetry: one campus has an
auditorium, another has developers, judges or 3D printers.
HOWCommunities list lendable venues, equipment and
people (judges, speakers, mentors, volunteers) with
availability. Others request bookings, and completed
exchanges earn Collab Credits.
Postgres tstzrange slots + an EXCLUDE constraint, so double-
booking is impossible at the database level

Club memory that survives graduation.
PROBLEMEvery year the entire leadership graduates, and

HOWA structured handover: a role-succession workflow, a

RAG over vault docs (pgvector + LLM) · term column on

Campus Pulse
The city's student life, live on a map.
PROBLEMStudents can't see what's happening around them
across campuses.
HOWA map and heat layer of events, open calls and active
communities in a chosen radius and time window ("this
weekend, within 8 km"), with travel time from your campus.
PostGIS + vector tiles (MapLibre) · server-side clustering · tiles

09 Campus Passport
One identity for every campus.
PROBLEMEvery event uses a different form, and proof of
participation is scattered.
HOWOne QR identity for registration and check-in at any
college. Check-ins add stamps and badges, and organizers
issue VERIFIABLE CERTIFICATES that anyone can validate
from a public link, ready to share on LinkedIn.
signed short-lived QR tokens (verifiable offline by the organizer

Ask CampusConnect
Talk to the graph in plain language.
PROBLEMFilters can't express "robotics clubs in Delhi that

HOWA natural-language assistant that turns questions into

cards, not paragraphs.
LLM tool-calling against a whitelisted query API (never raw SQL) ·

Architecture principles
1 · Modular monolith first
One codebase and one deploy for the core, with modules that
never touch each other's tables directly (enforced with lint rules
and separate schemas). Extracting a module later is a deployment
change, not a rewrite.
2 · Postgres is the truth; everything else is a
projection
Search indexes, feeds, counters and analytics are derived from
domain events and can be rebuilt by replaying. That kills a whole
class of consistency bugs.
3 · Event-driven by default
Every state change publishes a versioned domain event through a
transactional outbox. Consumers are idempotent, and nothing
slow (emails, AI, indexing) sits on the request path.
4 · Design for spikes, not averages
Fest launches and registration openings create 10–50× bursts. We
use predictive pre-scaling, a waiting room, atomic seat counters
and load shedding (§15).
5 · Privacy & safety by design
DPDP-aligned consent, data minimisation, minors' protections and
India data residency are part of the schema and the defaults, not a
later audit fix.
6 · AI with guardrails
Structured outputs validated against schemas, human
confirmation before anything publishes, per-tenant cost quotas
and a non-AI fallback for every AI feature.
Request lifecycle: "publish an event"
Client-perceived latency: about 120 ms (one DB transaction). Everything else completes asynchronously within seconds, and the event is searchable
in under 5 s (p95).

Collab Compass scoring
Match(A, B) = 0.30·sim(goalsA, goalsB) + 0.25·comp(skillsA, skillsB) + 0.15·geo(dAB) + 0.15·trust(B) +
0.10·activity(B) + 0.05·novelty(A, B) − clash(A, B)
comp = relevance-weighted (1 − sim(skills)) · geo(d) = e−d/15 km · novelty rewards first-time cross-college edges · clash
penalises calendar conflicts

Cell-based architecture (Stage 3): each "cell" is a full copy of the stack serving a set of regions (for example North, South, West, East), with a thin
global directory routing users to their home cell. Cross-cell objects such as national events and collaborations live in a global tier. A failure is

Surviving "Fest Rush"
Predictive pre-scaling
Registration opening times are known in advance, so the
scheduler pre-scales API and realtime pods and warms caches 10
minutes before every launch above a reach threshold. We don't
wait for autoscaling to react.
Virtual waiting room
For extreme launches, Cloudflare Waiting Room admits users at a
sustainable rate with signed admission tokens. The rest see a fair
queue position instead of errors.
Atomic seat inventory
Seats are decremented atomically in Redis (a Lua script), the
registration is confirmed asynchronously in Postgres, and a nightly
job reconciles the two. There's no overselling and no hot-row
locks.
Load shedding & degradation
Priority classes are registration and messaging, then feed, then
recommendations. Under pressure, recommendations fall back to
cached "trending" and non-critical work is deferred to queues.

Glossary
CDC: change data capture; streaming database changes from the write-
ahead log.
Outbox: a table written in the same transaction as business data, then
published as events.
RLS: row-level security; Postgres-enforced access rules per row.
ISR: incremental static regeneration; cached pages refreshed in the
background.
LTR: learning-to-rank; ML models that learn ordering from outcomes.
MMR: maximal marginal relevance; re-ranking for diversity.
RPO / RTO: how much data, and how much time, a disaster may cost.
DPDP: India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.
